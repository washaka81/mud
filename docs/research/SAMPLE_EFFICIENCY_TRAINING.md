# MUD Training: Hyper-Sample Efficiency (The 4 Pillars)
**Date**: 2026-07-13
**Objective**: Achieve maximum intelligence and model stabilization using the absolute minimum number of tokens during 1.58-bit Quantization-Aware Training (QAT).

## Background
Traditional Next-Token Prediction over massive generic text corpora requires millions or billions of tokens for the model to recover from "Ternary Shock" (the catastrophic loss of precision when compressing FP32/FP16 weights to 1.58-bit). For a local, resource-constrained engine like Forge LLM (MUD), this approach is fundamentally inefficient.

To maximize Sample Efficiency (Intelligence per Token), MUD relies on the **Intrinsic Alignment Protocol**, composed of four distinct architectural pillars.

---

## Pillar 1: Synthetic Self-Play (Autoentrenamiento Autónomo)
**Status**: Priority 38 (Partial implementation)
**Concept**: 
Instead of forcing the model to minimize Cross-Entropy on a static external corpus (`unified_corpus.txt`), the engine executes an autoregressive generation pass (sampling) to produce its own high-confidence syntactic chains. These chains are then fed back into the `SlimeBackward` pass for QAT.
**Why it requires fewer tokens**:
The model trains using its own "voice" and existing latent structural priors. The gradients do not fight against the pre-trained weights to memorize arbitrary external data; they merely refine, sharpen, and solidify the existing ternary mappings.

## Pillar 2: JEPA Lexical Resonance (Lexical Energy Prior)
**Status**: Priority 34 (Implemented)
**Concept**: 
Before the forward pass reaches the deep layers, the `SlimeRegister` initializes its internal JEPA attractor (`jepa_energy`) using the absolute magnitude of the input token's embedding vector. 
**Why it requires fewer tokens**:
This acts as a mathematical shield. By injecting the raw semantic "weight" of the word directly into the multiplicative control gate, the model instantly filters out the destructive noise generated by the 1.58-bit truncation. The model is saved from having to process millions of tokens just to "re-learn" vocabulary distributions.

## Pillar 3: Geometric Gradient Orthogonalization (Muon Optimizer)
**Status**: Priorities 41 & 43 (Implemented)
**Concept**: 
Standard Adam optimizer treats every parameter independently, which is slow and data-hungry. MUD maps optimizers dynamically based on matrix topology. For massive square matrices (e.g., `attn_q.weight`, `attn_output.weight`), the engine switches to the **Muon** optimizer, which utilizes Newton-Schulz iterations to orthogonalize the weight updates.
**Why it requires fewer tokens**:
Orthogonal updates guarantee that every token gradient extracts the maximum amount of non-redundant signal. Geometrically, the model traverses the loss landscape in straight lines rather than zigzagging, plummeting the required training time from 27h/epoch to under 10h/epoch.

## Pillar 4: Sampled Softmax with "Hard Negatives"
**Status**: Priority 32 (Implemented)
**Concept**: 
Instead of computing the exact Softmax distribution over all 49,152 or 128,256 vocabulary tokens (which dilutes the gradient and causes Catastrophic Weight Decay), MUD contrasts the target token against exactly 63 "Hard Negatives"—words that appeared recently in the context window.
**Why it requires fewer tokens**:
This transforms the objective from generic memorization into surgical discrimination. The embeddings develop hyper-specific boundaries to distinguish between semantically similar but incorrect words, requiring a fraction of the data compared to full-vocabulary Cross-Entropy.
